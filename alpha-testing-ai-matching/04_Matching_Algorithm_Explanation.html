Matching Algorithm Explanation

Implementation audit | Exact rules, data flow and known gaps

Source inspection date: 17 November 2026. This package does not change code, database contents or application behavior. Expected rule outcomes are calculated with imported pure functions; deployed feature correctness has not been established.

Implemented architecture

The feature combines deterministic scoring with two optional Gemini services. Analyze submits criteria, course, notes, computed level, selected teacher, birthday and optional schedule to POST /api/admin/recommend-teacher. deriveMatchInsights maps notes to allowed criteria. recommendTeacher loads the active course pool, scores candidates, optionally applies advisor order, then applies availability, child suitability and selection policy.

The form displays a single recommended teacher with reasons, coverage and warnings and fills Assigned Teacher. The response also contains rankedTeachers; the inspected UI does not show a full ranked score table. Save uses POST /api/admin/users and recalculates matching before persisting the final assignment.

Critical AI data-contract finding

backend/teacherMatching.js::scoreTeacherForStudent parses raw bio and specialization internally but returns the raw teacher plus score, criteria coverage and reasons, with no nested profile. backend/server.js::scoreWith spreads that result and adds availability. backend/teacherAdvisor.js::describeCandidates reads teacher.profile || {} and then reads profile.specialization, teachingStyle, idealStudentPace, experienceYears and bio.

A local, side-effect-free reproduction calls the actual scorer and the extracted describeCandidates function with T01. The adapter receives specialization [], teachingStyle [], bestStudentPace [], experienceYears null and bioExcerpt "", even though T01 has all those attributes in the raw row. ID, name, active load and availability still reach the adapter.

Impact: current live AI may rank candidates without the intended teaching attributes. Model completion, a displayed explanation or advisor.used = true does not prove a suitable match. TC17 must assess explanation truthfulness against stored profiles. This finding is documented, not fixed.

Proficiency in the AI branch

adviseTeacherRanking receives criteria, trialNotes, courseName, scored rows and availability, but no proficiencyLevel argument. buildPrompt lists four preference categories and notes, and does not include the rule score. Level can affect rules ordering and a capped shortlist, but does not provide an explicit proficiency instruction to the model. Notes may incidentally mention a level; that is not a direct level contract.

Other interpretation limits

Student trial notes are ignored by the deterministic score directly. They can affect matching through AI-derived criteria and through the advisor prompt. Teacher-name tokens are excluded from the weak free-text signal. A score is not a probability. Coverage reflects four categories, not each selected option and not proficiency.

Level computation and weighting

Rubric keys: speakingPronunciation, readingComprehension, structure, sentenceGrammar, vocabulary. Enter complete integer values 1–5. Rules are evaluated in the following order; the first match wins.

RuleComputed level
Any core reading/structure/grammar/vocabulary score = 1Novice (stored novice-low)
Every score >= 4 and mean >= 4.5Advanced
Every score >= 3 and mean >= 3.5Intermediate High
Every score >= 3Intermediate Mid
Otherwise mean >= 2.8Intermediate Mid
OtherwiseIntermediate Low

Experience minimum by stored level: Novice 0, Intermediate Low 1, Intermediate Mid 2, Intermediate High 3, Advanced 5 years. For levels needing more than zero years: sufficient experience adds 6; a shortfall subtracts 2 per missing year. This is a score adjustment, not a minimum-experience exclusion.

Complete deterministic score

2 × min(experience years, 10) + 14 for selected-course match + up to 2 goal hits × 12 + up to 2 focus hits × 10 + up to 2 style hits × 10 + up to 2 pace hits × 8 + proficiency adjustment + up to 2 teacher-prose category signals × 3 − 2 × active assigned students. Values are normalized/parsed as implemented in teacherMatching.js.

rankTeachers sorts descending score, then ascending load. Equal score/load retains input order; the SQL pool is ordered by first and last name. Selection prefers below-two-load candidates, then least loaded when all are at the target. The manual explicit-pick path can override the target and warns.

Pool restrictions and additional factors

The query restricts users.role = teacher, users.status = active, and selected course membership in teacher_courses. It intentionally does not exclude incomplete teacher profiles. Workload counts only assigned students whose user account is active and profile_completed = TRUE. It is not class bookings or package count.

preferAvailable stably moves teachers with at least one suitable upcoming date first, after AI ordering. Availability uses teacher_availability per date, scheduled classes, breaks and class duration across a 21-day window. UI periods are Morning 06:00–12:00, Afternoon 12:00–17:00 and Evening 17:00–24:00. No preference means no availability query; no match still yields a recommendation with conflict information.

preferTeachesChildren is applied after availability for known age <= 12. It moves teachers who accept children ahead of opt-outs. Missing birthday is reported unknown. If everyone opts out, a recommendation can still be returned with childAgeConflict; a manual/load policy can also affect final selection. Do not describe these as absolute exclusion filters.

The AI advisor caps its strongest rules shortlist at 60, temperature 0.2; insights uses 0.1. Invalid/unrecognized candidate IDs are discarded; unmentioned eligible candidates remain in the order. Configuration, timeouts, bad responses and service failures trigger rules-only operation. Transport failures can open the process-local advisor circuit after three failures for five minutes.

Coverage and saving behavior

assessMatchStrength: all selected categories matched = strong; at least half = partial; less than half = weak; zero categories selected = unknown. Four-category coverage can be strong even if not every item in a multi-select category overlaps. Creation requires complete rubric scores; backend recomputes the saved level. Analyze uses the supplied level normalized by the server, so a successful preview is not proof of the save path.
